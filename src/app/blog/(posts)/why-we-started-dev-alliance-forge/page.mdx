Most of us met the same way — stuck on something, searching for an answer, finding a tutorial
written for a completely different context, and having no one nearby to actually ask.

That's the gap Dev Alliance Forge exists to close. Not a lack of resources — the internet has no
shortage of tutorials — but a lack of *people*. Someone to review your first pull request and
actually explain what to fix, not just approve or reject it. Someone to tell you which of the ten
conflicting tutorials is actually still accurate. Someone who's been stuck exactly where you are
and got unstuck.

## Three things we kept coming back to

Every time we talked about what a community like this should actually do, we kept landing on the
same three ideas:

- **Forge skills** — not by producing more content, but by getting people building real things,
  with real feedback, faster than they would alone.
- **Mentor each other** — explicitly two-way. The person mentoring today was being mentored six
  months ago, and will be again on something else next month.
- **Bridge standards** — global best practices are usually written assuming context (tools,
  infrastructure, hiring norms) that doesn't always match ours. Someone has to do the translation.

## What this looks like in practice

In practice, it's build nights where nobody's expected to already know the answer. It's workshops
run by whoever in the community is a few steps ahead on that particular topic, not by a single
designated expert. It's open-source sprints where "first PR ever" and "maintainer" work side by
side on the same board.

None of this required inventing anything new — just committing to showing up for each other
consistently. That's genuinely most of what Dev Alliance Forge is.
